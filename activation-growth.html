Why activation mattered

Part3's growth depended on firms adopting the product across more users and projects. But getting teams started often required significant support from CX.

We wanted to reduce time to first value (TTFV), how long customers took to complete their first meaningful workflow, and time to activation (TTA), how long firms took to reach broader adoption.

The goal was to help firms get started independently, reduce the need for one-to-one training, and support growth without increasing the workload on CX.

Customer journey: first project to continued adoption

What we learned

I worked with CX to observe onboarding sessions, review training calls and support requests, and analyze customer behaviour.

We found recurring friction in team setup, reviewer assignments, and project configuration. Customers often needed CX to help them complete these steps before they could begin work.

We also lacked a shared definition of activation, reliable baseline data, and consistent tracking across onboarding. We knew where individual customers struggled, but couldn't measure how firms progressed or identify which problems to address first.

Product direction

Rather than introducing more onboarding instructions, we focused on making the workflows customers already needed to complete easier.

Working with CX and engineering, I prioritized opportunities based on customer impact, time to first value, and implementation effort.

We focused on helping firms start work sooner and making it easier to start subsequent projects. Alongside these improvements, we defined how to measure activation and identify where to optimize next.

Activation journey with prioritized opportunities

What shipped · 01

Team setup

Hypothesis: If we simplify team setup and reviewer assignments, more firms will complete their first workflow without CX assistance, reducing time to first value.

Approach: I redesigned team setup to support contact imports, bulk adding, existing-contact detection, and automatic reviewer assignments based on discipline.

Result: Firms could add multiple consultants at once, while common reviewer assignments were handled during setup instead of configured individually.

Animation: import contacts → detect existing → add team → populate reviewers

What shipped · 02

Project templates

Hypothesis: If firms can reuse project configurations, more will start subsequent projects independently, increasing repeat adoption and reducing reliance on CX.

Approach: I designed organization-level templates that allowed firms to save and reuse project settings while customizing individual projects.

Result: 77 projects started from firm-authored templates. Among firms that created a template, 54% of their subsequent projects reused one.

Animation: create template → reuse configuration → start new project

What shipped · 03

Measuring activation

Hypothesis: If we define clear activation criteria and track customer progress, we can establish a baseline, identify where firms struggle, and decide what to improve next.

Approach: I defined activation milestones, criteria for first value, and a reporting framework. Working with CX, we also separated self-led from CX-guided onboarding to understand how customers progressed.

Result: We established a historical 37% activation baseline and a 14-day TTFV target. This gave the team a consistent way to track progress, identify drop-offs, and prioritize future improvements.

Activation funnel → criteria → baseline and TTFV tracking

Early outcomes

The work reduced manual steps in onboarding, introduced reusable project setup, and established a consistent way to measure activation.

Internal reporting also suggested shorter times to first value:

A later data audit could not consistently reproduce these comparisons, so we could not confirm the reductions or attribute them directly to the shipped changes.

Beyond the product improvements, we established a clearer definition of activation and a way to measure customer progress. This gave the team a better foundation for deciding what to improve next.
