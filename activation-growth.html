Activation & Growth

Role
Product Design Lead · Growth PM
Team
Customer Experience, Engineering, Data
Timeline
Q2 2026

Part3 grew through a land-and-expand sales model supported by white-glove customer onboarding. As the customer base expanded, relying on CX to get every firm up and running became difficult to scale.

I took ownership of activation across product strategy and design, leading the initiative alongside a Customer Success Manager. I helped set growth priorities, designed onboarding improvements, and defined how we would measure progress.

Our focus was self-led activation: helping firms get started and reach value through the product, without requiring CX-led setup and training.

Why activation mattered

Part3's growth depended on firms adopting the product across more users and projects. But getting teams started often required significant support from CX.

We wanted to reduce time to first value (TTFV), the time it takes customers to complete their first meaningful workflow, and time to activation (TTA), the time it takes firms to reach broader adoption.

The goal was to make self-led onboarding a viable path for more customers, reducing the need for one-to-one training while supporting growth without increasing the workload on CX.

Activation funnel showing where customers needed CX support and which opportunities we prioritized

What we learned

I worked with CX to observe onboarding sessions, review training calls and support requests, and analyze customer behaviour.

We found recurring friction in team setup, reviewer assignments, and project configuration. Customers often needed CX to help them complete these steps before they could begin work.

We also lacked a shared definition of activation, reliable baseline data, and consistent tracking across onboarding. We knew where individual customers struggled, but couldn't measure how firms progressed or identify which problems to address first.

Approach

Before we could measure improvement, we needed to define what activation meant for Part3.

I worked with CX to establish milestones for first value and broader adoption, then partnered with engineering to build an initial activation dashboard.

We separated self-led and CX-guided onboarding to understand how customers progressed through each path. While both groups used the same product, we prioritized changes that would help self-led customers complete setup and begin work independently.

Rather than introducing more onboarding instructions, we focused on making existing workflows easier. We prioritized opportunities based on customer impact, time to first value, and implementation effort.

Activation journey with prioritized opportunities

01. Team setup

Hypothesis: If we simplify team setup and reviewer assignments, more firms will complete their first workflow without CX assistance, reducing time to first value.

Approach: I redesigned team setup to support contact imports, bulk adding, existing-contact detection, and automatic reviewer assignments based on discipline.

Result: The redesigned Teams experience shipped with bulk adding, contact reuse, and automatic reviewer assignments. This removed repetitive configuration steps from the initial setup process.

Import contacts → detect existing → add team → populate reviewers

02. Project templates

Hypothesis: If firms can reuse project configurations, more will start subsequent projects independently, increasing repeat adoption and reducing reliance on CX.

Approach: I designed organization-level templates that allowed firms to save and reuse project settings while customizing individual projects.

Result: 77 projects started from firm-authored templates. Among firms that created a template, 54% of their subsequent projects reused one.

Create template → reuse configuration → start new project

03. Measuring activation

Hypothesis: If we define clear activation criteria and track customer progress, we can establish a baseline, identify where firms struggle, and decide what to improve next.

Approach: I defined activation milestones, criteria for first value, and a reporting framework. Working with CX, we separated self-led from CX-guided onboarding to understand how customers progressed.

Result: We established a historical first-distribution baseline and a 14-day TTFV target, and built an activation dashboard to track customer progress. Product and CX could now evaluate onboarding using shared metrics instead of relying solely on individual customer reports.
