AI & Analytics · Part3

Construction Data & Insights

Role
Product Design Lead
Team
Product, Engineering, Leadership
Timeline
Q4 2026

Architects use Part3 to manage construction administration, from reviewing submittals and responding to RFIs to tracking changes and drawing revisions. These workflows generate valuable information about project progress, turnaround times, and outstanding work.

But while Part3 helped teams manage their work, customers had limited control over how they could analyze and report on it.

I identified an opportunity to turn that data into more useful insights, defining a customizable dashboard experience with AI-assisted reporting.

Why reporting mattered

Part3 already offered project dashboards and cross-project summaries, but reporting was largely predefined. Firms needed different information depending on their projects, teams, and responsibilities.

Customer requests revealed three recurring needs:

  • 01Relevant metricsPrioritize overdue work, turnaround times, and accountability instead of relying on fixed charts.
  • 02Cross-project visibilityCompare performance across teams, disciplines, and projects.
  • 03Flexible reportingCreate views tailored to specific needs and communicate results to leadership.

These limitations often pushed customers toward spreadsheets, exports, and additional reporting tools.

For Part3, improving analytics offered an opportunity to support more of the decisions firms make beyond day-to-day document management.

Existing dashboard and anonymized customer requests

What MCP revealed

With expanded Postgres-backed data infrastructure, we introduced a Model Context Protocol (MCP) integration that enabled customers to analyze project information through external AI tools.

Early use cases included turnaround benchmarking, workload analysis, financial reporting, and performance dashboards.

These examples showed what more flexible reporting could offer, but accessing those insights still required technical setup and familiarity with external tools.

The opportunity was to bring that flexibility directly into Part3, without requiring customers to understand the technology behind it.

Project data → MCP reporting → in-product dashboard

Product direction

The challenge was balancing flexibility with accessibility.

A chat-first experience could support complex questions but required users to know what to ask. A traditional dashboard was familiar but couldn't accommodate every firm's reporting needs.

Working across Product and Engineering, I advocated for a visual-first approach: customizable dashboards that showed useful information immediately, with AI available to investigate results and create reports.

This gave AI a specific purpose. Rather than generating the numbers, AI would reduce the effort needed to explore and organize data that Part3 already held.

Dashboard → investigate → create

Design decisions · 01

Making reporting customizable

Hypothesis: Giving customers control over their reporting would increase its relevance across different roles, encouraging repeat use and reducing reliance on manual workarounds.

Approach: I explored suggested charts with configurable metrics, groupings, filters, and layouts. Customers could build views around project performance or compare results across their organization.

Suggested charts reduced the effort required to get started, while customization allowed firms to measure what mattered to them.

Design outcome: A flexible reporting experience that supported different needs without requiring customers to build everything from scratch.

Animation: populated dashboard → add chart → configure → save

Design decisions · 02

Moving from insight to investigation

Hypothesis: Connecting metrics to their underlying records would help customers investigate problems faster and make decisions with greater confidence.

Approach: Building on our AI strategy discussions, I explored contextual actions that allowed users to ask questions about a chart, review a breakdown, and open supporting project records.

The key decision was to make answers verifiable. AI could help explain a trend, but customers needed access to the evidence behind it.

Design outcome: A direct path from spotting a performance issue to investigating its underlying cause.

Animation: chart → ask why → breakdown → source record

Design decisions · 03

Creating reports through conversation

Hypothesis: Natural-language report creation would reduce the effort required for custom reporting, allowing more customers to answer questions independently.

Approach: Working within Engineering's charting capabilities, I explored a workflow where customers could describe the report they needed, review the proposed chart, and add it to their dashboard.

AI would interpret the request and configure a supported visualization, while the underlying values came from Part3's data APIs.

Design outcome: A simpler way to create reusable reports without relying on external tools or manually configuring every chart.

Animation: request chart → preview → confirm → add to dashboard

Why it matters

MCP-powered reporting demonstrated how deeper access to construction data could support customer decisions.

Ryder ArchitectureTurnaround benchmarking

Ryder used its project data to compare review times against an industry benchmark, demonstrating the value of reporting beyond standard project summaries.

Parkin ArchitectsFinancial visibility

An MCP-powered dashboard surfaced outstanding pricing that informed the firm's project budget discussions. Parkin also wanted reporting to be more accessible to people across the firm.

BWASoftware consolidation

A performance benchmark showed BWA's turnaround times were 43% faster than peers. The analysis helped demonstrate the value of Part3 as the firm evaluated consolidating its construction administration software.

These use cases showed that more accessible reporting could support performance reviews, financial decisions, and software investment.

The dashboard direction built on those findings, combining customizable views, source-backed investigation, and conversational reporting. For Part3, it created an opportunity to make advanced analytics useful to more customers and strengthen the platform's value across an entire firm.
