Context

A user segment we never designed for

Our platform is built for architects managing construction administration. But general contractors (GCs) were showing up too. Invited by their architect clients, they were creating submittals in the product without onboarding, without guidance, and with no dedicated experience.

A data pull in early 2026 made the scale visible. GCs had become significant enough to shape the quality of the entire workflow.

18%

of all 2026 submittals created by GCs, a segment we had not intentionally designed for

4,312

submittals since January 1, 2026, running at roughly 2,000 per month

237

unique GC users across 143 companies, from small shops to $3B+ firms

30%

of GC users had created 20 or more submittals each, a highly active core

Problem

Four errors. All preventable.

Discovery interviews and support data revealed the same mistakes happening across GC users. Not because GCs were careless, but because the product gave them no guardrails.

01
No reviewer assigned

Submittals sit unreviewed for weeks. Architects don't get notified. Nobody finds out until it's late.

02
Informal titles and missing spec codes

GCs name submittals casually. Without CSI division codes, architects can't search, filter, or route documents correctly.

03
Misrouted submittals

Mechanical drawings sent to the electrical engineer. Wrong reviewer, wasted cycle, late catch.

04
Double-tracking in Excel

GC project managers maintain their own logs in spreadsheets because the in-app view doesn't give them enough visibility.

"Nobody was even aware it was uploaded. We would find out weeks later."

Insight

Don't change the behavior. Catch the error.

GCs are task-focused. They upload, they submit, they move on. Any solution requiring new habits or extra steps would be abandoned immediately.

The design challenge: eliminate errors automatically, and surface anything unresolved as context for the architect. Not a gate. A handoff.

01
Stripped-down GC view

Title, reviewer, files, quality check. No architecture-specific concepts. Simpler than the main product by design.

02
AI auto-fill from the PDF

The document already has the right information. AI reads the cover page and fills in division code, spec title, and reference number automatically.

03
Flag, don't block

Hard gates cause drop-off. Unresolved issues become contextual review flags for the architect instead of walls for the GC.

04
Bridge both sides

What the GC skipped, the architect sees. One continuous record instead of two separate experiences.

Design

Three directions. One recommendation.

I stress-tested each option against a single question: does this work if the GC does the minimum possible?

Option 1 Recommended

Quality Check on Draft State

AI pre-check runs on upload. Results surface at submit time, not during creation. Works whether or not the GC engages with it. Unaddressed issues carry forward as architect review flags.

Option 2

Two-Step Creation Wizard

Enforced gate: create details, then see the pre-check. Guarantees visibility but adds friction. Risk: GCs treat Step 2 as a click-through.

Option 3

Inline Pre-Check

Quality flags surface inline during creation. Lower friction than Option 2. The carry-forward mechanism here is the key idea worth keeping regardless of which direction ships.

Outcome

Where it stands

The project is mid-shaping and targeting a June 2026 beta. The design work moved faster than expected because the data made the case before any mockup was shown. Stakeholders aligned quickly on scope and direction.

What landed
  • Data-first framing compressed alignment. The 18% stat made the problem real before any design was shared.
  • An interactive POC replaced lengthy spec debates. People responded to something they could click.
  • The carry-forward flag mechanism reframed the tradeoff. No hard gate needed if issues stay visible downstream.
  • Three parallel directions surfaced tradeoffs that a single-option PRD would have hidden.
What is still open
  • GC behavioral data is thin. Seven interviews, limited in-product analytics for this segment.
  • The default reviewer pattern needs validation before shipping. Auto-assigning may create noise for some firms.
  • Multi-prime projects, where multiple GCs work the same job, are not fully addressed yet.
  • Intentional GC activation post-launch is undefined. Adoption has been organic and passive.
Reflection

What I took from this

01

The user who didn't choose you is still your user

GCs didn't buy this product. Their clients did. The design contract is different. The bar is not delight — it is: don't make it worse than doing nothing.

02

The best AI features remove errors before they happen

Auto-filling metadata from a PDF cover page is not flashy. But it eliminates the single biggest GC error without asking anyone to change their behavior. That is the kind of automation worth building.

03

This is our first intentional design for a non-architect persona

Everything we had built assumed the architect is the primary user. GC Pre-Review is the first time we asked what the experience looks like from the other side of the table. That question has implications beyond this feature.
