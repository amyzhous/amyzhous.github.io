We weren't building another file browser.

Turning a file-management brief into a new paid product for managing construction drawings.

58K+ drawings managed · 0→1 paid product launched

Role Led end-to-end product design, from information architecture and interaction design to launch support and growth.

A different problem was hiding inside the brief.

Project Files began as a straightforward request: give architects somewhere to organize and upload their files.

But architects already had places to store PDFs. What they couldn't easily manage was the information changing inside those files. Drawings were revised, reissued, and shared throughout construction, often across multiple tools.

Working with product and engineering, I helped shift the direction from generic file management to drawing management.

The important distinction was simple: a PDF is a file, but a drawing has an identity and history.

Making a drawing set manageable

Construction teams often receive hundreds of sheets inside a combined PDF. Asking architects to split, name, and organize every page manually would make the product feel like more work than the tools they already used.

I designed an import experience that extracts individual sheets and their details, then lets users review the results and correct mistakes before publishing.

The tradeoff was between automation and control. We wanted to remove repetitive setup without asking architects to trust every extracted value.

Keeping the current drawing connected to its history

Version control became more complicated once drawings were connected to the rest of construction administration.

A sheet needed a stable identity across revisions, but the same identifier could appear in different drawing packages. Teams also needed to understand which document had issued an update.

I designed the revision experience around those relationships rather than treating every upload as a separate file.

The result was a drawing register that could show what was current without losing the record of how it changed.

From product launch to product adoption

Project Files launched as a paid product area, with customers now managing 58K+ drawings in Part3.

The structured drawing model also gave the team a foundation for revision comparison, drawing references, and specifications.

But launch raised a second challenge. Uploads and purchases showed demand; they didn't tell us whether teams were regularly using drawings throughout construction.

That distinction continues to shape how I think about the product: storing the latest information is only the starting point. The value comes from making it useful in the work people do every day.
