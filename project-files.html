Why Project Files?

Architects relied on drawings to review submittals, answer RFIs, and issue changes, but managed them in separate tools. Teams had to switch between products to find drawings, check revisions, and complete their work.

For Part3, this exposed a gap in the existing product and an opportunity to introduce a new paid offering.

Product direction

Project Files began as a file-management concept. Through customer discovery and workflow analysis, I found that a general file library could store PDFs, but couldn't help architects identify current drawings or track revisions.

I worked with product and engineering to shift the scope toward drawing management. We defined a model built around drawing sets, individual sheets, and revision history, with specifications organized into divisions and sections.

We prioritized two workflows: importing large drawing sets with minimal setup and managing current revisions throughout construction.

Importing drawings

Drawing sets often arrive as a single PDF containing hundreds of sheets. Requiring architects to split and label them manually would make adopting Project Files more difficult.

We chose to extract sheet numbers, titles, and disciplines automatically, then let users review the results before publishing.

The extra review step was deliberate. It added a little time to setup but prevented inaccurate sheet information from carrying into the drawing register.

Ask about an issued set

Ask which sheets cover a floor and get an answer cited to the drawings it came from.

Ask about a specification division

Ask a question of one division and see what it requires, without opening the set.

Managing revisions

The newest upload isn't always the latest issued drawing. Automatic versioning alone couldn't account for historical uploads or different issue dates.

We combined automatic revision tracking with manual control, so teams could identify current drawings without losing their revision history.

Launch

Project Files shipped as a paid product for architecture firms, supporting drawing management, current-set exports, and references across construction administration workflows. The product went on to generate $652K+ in associated annual revenue, with more than 58,000 drawings managed in Part3.
