Self-Serve Billing

Role
Product Design Lead
Team

The person adding a teammate wasn't the person paying for the seat.

Customers could manage their projects independently, but expanding their teams still required support. I designed the self-serve experience to remove that dependency while giving firms control over additional spending.

Why billing mattered

When firms reached their seat limit, administrators encountered an overage warning, and additional access required coordination with internal operations and billing teams.

For customers, this delayed onboarding and made it harder to bring collaborators into active projects. It also created friction in self-led activation, where getting teams working is essential to reaching first value.

For the business, manual seat expansion increased operational overhead and introduced unnecessary friction at a natural point of growth.

The challenge was to remove that dependency without giving every administrator authority to approve additional spending.

Product direction

Our initial approach required billing approval before granting access. While it protected spending decisions, it also made an employee's ability to work dependent on the availability of an approver.

During implementation, we shifted to provision first, approve second. New members could begin working immediately while billing approval happened within a seven-day window.

This was more than a change in sequence. It separated access from purchasing approval, allowing teams to continue working while preserving financial oversight.

It also introduced a new permissions model where access could be provisional, approved, or downgraded. My focus was translating that model into an experience that worked for both employees and billing administrators.

Design decisions · 01

Designing for two decision-makers

Employees and billing administrators had different responsibilities, so they needed different information.

I designed role-specific experiences that made those responsibilities explicit. Employees could understand their current access, approval status, and deadline. Billing administrators could review pending requests, assess their financial implications, and take action.

The goal was to keep approvals visible without making them a prerequisite for starting work.

Design decisions · 02

Managing provisional access

Provision-first introduced an important tradeoff. Removing employees when approval expired could disrupt project collaboration, but leaving their access unchanged indefinitely would undermine financial controls.

We used a seven-day approval window, after which unapproved permissions automatically stepped down to Viewer.

The member remained on the project, and the request stayed open so approval could restore their original access without another invitation.

Expired access: employee and billing-admin views

What it changed

Most recorded activity involved approving additional access rather than purchasing seats directly. This suggested that the approval experience was more central to how firms expanded their teams than checkout alone.

Self-serve billing moved routine seat expansion into the product, reducing reliance on manual operations and allowing customers to manage growth more independently.

For the business, it established a more scalable expansion process. For customers, it removed a dependency that could delay getting the right people onto a project.
