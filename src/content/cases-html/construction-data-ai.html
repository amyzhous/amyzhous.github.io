Overview

Firms had the data, but not the reports they needed.

Part3 stores years of construction records, from drawings and submittals to RFIs and project changes.

Customers repeatedly asked for more flexible reporting than our dashboards could provide.

Hero: customizable construction analytics dashboard

Product preview

Introducing Insights Builder.

Insights Builder gives teams dashboards they can adapt to their work. They can add and arrange charts, save their views, and share reports with colleagues.

Ask Part3 is the assistant built into Part3.

It uses the current page or selected chart as context, so people can ask questions, create charts, and check the records behind a result.

MCP usage

Customers were building the reports we had not provided.

Part3 had already released MCP, which lets firms access Part3 data through external AI tools like Claude. Some used it to create charts our dashboards did not provide.

The examples matched reporting needs we had heard repeatedly in customer research.

What the examples showed. Different firms were asking different questions: review performance, project health, and work still outstanding. One fixed set of reports could not cover them all.

The product limitation. When we decided which reports to build, we also decided which questions customers could answer. Every new need started the cycle again.

From evidence to direction

Stop defining every report for customers.

I connected earlier customer research with the reports firms were creating through MCP. Working with the engineer leading MCP, I helped define the shift from predefined reports to configurable reporting in Part3.

Rather than build a report for every request, we proposed giving firms control over reporting in Part3. They could answer different questions without waiting for Part3 to build each report.

Why reporting mattered

Different roles needed different answers.

On a project: which items are delaying a response? CA leads needed to see overdue items, reviewers, and delays on their projects.

Across the firm: where is time being lost or saved? Directors needed to compare performance across projects and spot recurring issues.

Design decision 01

Show the dashboard first, then help people investigate.

A conversational interface requires users to know what to ask. CA teams needed to spot overdue work before investigating why, so we led with key metrics.

Ask Part3 used the selected chart as context and linked to source records. Using it across Part3 avoided a separate AI workflow for each feature.

Animation: Select chart → ask why → review breakdown → open source records

Design decision 02

Give customers control without requiring setup.

Firms and roles needed different reports. Building predefined charts for every request kept Part3 in control and added maintenance work. We provided useful default views, then let users select or create charts through Ask Part3. Flexibility adds complexity, so configuration stayed optional.

Animation: Describe report → review chart preview → confirm → save to dashboard

Design decision 03

Use AI to define the report, not the numbers.

AI could help customers ask for a report in their own words. But a convincing chart is risky if its metric, scope, or data is wrong. We needed to separate the flexibility of the request from the reliability of the result.

Our design direction was to use AI to interpret reporting intent, while relying on defined calculations over Part3 records for the figures. The interface also needed to show what the result covered and let users inspect the source.

  • Interpret

    Clarify the metric, timeframe, and project scope

  • Calculate

    Ground figures in Part3 records and defined rules

  • Verify

    Expose filters and the records behind a chart

Business opportunity

Usability, flexibility, and scalability

Some people needed a quick overview. Others had questions our standard reports could not answer. The design had to serve both without making everyone configure their dashboards.

Self-serve reporting could reduce one-off requests to CX and engineering. That would leave more time for work customers could not handle themselves. We wanted to validate this benefit after launch.

  • Usability

    See overdue work and review trends without setup

  • Flexibility

    Build reports around each firm's questions

  • Scalability

    Meet new reporting needs without building each report

Evaluation

How we would evaluate the product strategy

  • 01Independent use

    Can firms answer new questions without our help?

    Track repeat reporting and CX requests. If firms still need help, find which questions the product cannot answer.

  • 02Defaults vs. control

    Where do standard views stop being useful?

    Compare default views with custom charts across roles. Low customization may mean the defaults already work.

  • 03Trust in answers

    Can teams verify what a chart is telling them?

    Check whether people open source records when results look wrong. Improve the path from chart to evidence.

  • 04Operating leverage

    Can reporting scale without more one-off work?

    Compare reporting demand with CX and engineering effort. Prioritize recurring gaps over one-off requests.

Reflection

The hard part was deciding what Part3 should decide.

With fixed reports, Part3 decided what customers could see. A fully open builder would make everyone configure their own reporting. We chose useful defaults with room to adapt, so firms could answer different questions without starting from scratch.
