# What if reporting didn't require knowing what to ask?

**Exploring a visual-first approach to construction analytics, with AI available when users need to go deeper.**

**Outcome: Product direction proposed to leadership · Internal dashboard builder demonstrated**

**Role:** Product concept and interaction design exploration. Engineering developed the internal dashboard implementation.

[HERO — Full-width dashboard with meaningful construction metrics, charts, and a contextual AI panel.]

## Powerful data was still difficult to use.

Part3 already captured information about submittals, RFIs, reviews, and project activity. But turning that information into useful reporting wasn't straightforward.

Advanced integrations and AI tools offered new possibilities, but they also assumed users knew what information to request.

I proposed a different starting point: give users a useful view of their data first, then let them ask questions or build on it.

[STATIC — Dashboard overview, highlighting useful default metrics and the contextual Ask entry point.]

## Start with something people can understand.

Not every user wants to describe a report in a prompt.

I explored a dashboard experience where project teams could browse suggested charts, choose what to measure, and adjust the grouping or time period.

The interface makes those choices visible instead of hiding them inside a conversation.

[ANIMATION — Browse charts → Configure measure and grouping → Add to dashboard.]

## Use AI to investigate, not replace the evidence.

Once someone spots a trend, the next question is often why it happened.

I explored contextual questions directly from a chart, allowing users to examine a change and follow the answer back to the underlying project records.

The important distinction was between helping users interpret a question and giving them a result they could verify.

[HERO ANIMATION — Select review-time trend → Ask why it increased → View breakdown → Open source record.]

[STATIC CLOSE-UP — AI answer beside its supporting records.]

## Let people create without learning a reporting tool.

Natural language offered another way to create charts. Rather than generating a final dashboard without review, the proposed interaction let users inspect the chart's scope and destination before adding it.

[STATIC — Chat-generated chart preview, showing metric, grouping, date range, and confirmation.]

The dashboard direction was presented to leadership and followed by an internal engineering preview. The implementation uses Part3's existing data APIs, with deterministic metrics rather than AI-generated numbers.

The product was still in development at this stage, so customer adoption and effectiveness remain unmeasured.

**The opportunity:** Make more of Part3's existing data useful to the people managing projects, without requiring them to become reporting experts.

Why this works: The story demonstrates product vision through an actual interaction model. It avoids presenting AI as the achievement in itself.
